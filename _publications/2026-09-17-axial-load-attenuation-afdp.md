## Abstract

Ultrashort-radius horizontal drilling uses articulated flexible drillpipe to achieve build radii below 3 m, but axial-load attenuation can limit horizontal reach and reduce weight on bit. This study develops a multibody dynamics model of the horizontal section, representing the articulated pipe as rigid segments connected by constant-velocity universal joints with hard-stop angle limits. The formulation incorporates nonlinear Hertzian contact and regularized, velocity-dependent friction.

The model is benchmarked against analytical and soft-string solutions, verified by timestep refinement for transient joint-limit activation, and cross-verified against an independent ADAMS simulation. The simulations reproduce helical attitude, backward whirl, precession, and end-face-edge contact. Results show that when the joint deflection limit is below a geometric critical angle, the confined pipe evolves into a discrete helical configuration under axial compression. This configuration localizes contact at end-face edges and increases the mean joint normal force by more than an order of magnitude relative to an equivalent continuous beam.

Parametric simulations show that rotational speed increases relative slip velocity, while local axial loading mainly suppresses whirling frequency without substantially increasing slip. Higher friction moves the response closer to the theoretical constrained-whirling condition and shifts frictional dissipation toward coupled axial-circumferential motion. For a 12 m near-bit section, axial-force loss reaches approximately 38 kN and torque loss approximately 800 N·m, with torque dissipation about eight times that of an equivalent continuous beam in the studied case. These results provide a mechanics-based basis for articulated flexible drillpipe design and operating-parameter optimization in ultrashort-radius horizontal wells.
