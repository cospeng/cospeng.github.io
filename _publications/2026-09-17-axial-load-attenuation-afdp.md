## Summary

Ultrashort-radius horizontal drilling uses articulated flexible drillpipe to achieve build radii below 3 m, but axial-load attenuation can limit horizontal reach and reduce weight on bit. This study develops a multibody dynamics model of the horizontal section, representing the articulated pipe as rigid segments connected by constant-velocity universal joints with hard-stop angle limits. The formulation incorporates nonlinear Hertzian contact and regularized, velocity-dependent friction.

The model is benchmarked against analytical and soft-string solutions, refined for timestep sensitivity, and cross-verified against an independent ADAMS simulation. The simulations reproduce helical attitude, backward whirl, precession, and end-face-edge contact. Results show that a geometry-induced discrete helical configuration can localize contact and substantially increase joint normal force relative to an equivalent continuous beam.

Parametric simulations further show how rotational speed, axial loading, and friction affect slip and whirling. For a 12 m near-bit section, the studied case exhibits approximately 38 kN axial-force loss and 800 N·m torque loss, providing a mechanics-based basis for articulated-pipe design and operating-parameter optimization.
